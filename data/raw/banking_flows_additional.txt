Internal operations notes for the lending and customer platform.

When a new customer arrives at the branch or opens a digital onboarding flow,
the service desk verifies identity, captures the customer profile, and checks
whether the person already exists in the customer master. If the customer is
new, the platform creates a customer record, links identification documents,
and records consent. If the customer is returning, the agent updates the
address, phone number, employment status, and preferred contact channel.

Loan origination begins when a customer asks for a new personal loan or a
refinance. The advisor captures the requested amount, purpose, term, income
information, and the expected monthly payment. The application is checked
against policy rules, affordability rules, and risk thresholds. If data is
missing, the user task requests additional documents such as payslips, bank
statements, proof of residence, or employer details. The underwriting team
reviews the case and either approves, rejects, or sends it back for more
information. Once approved, the platform creates the loan account and stores
the amortization schedule.

Loan disbursement happens after approval and after all conditions precedent are
confirmed. The back office confirms the destination account, validates the
beneficiary, and schedules the transfer. In some cases the money goes to the
customer's own checking account; in other cases it pays a third party such as a
merchant, vehicle seller, or another financial institution. The system records
the disbursement date, the disbursed amount, and the funding source.

Loan payment operations happen every month or whenever the customer makes an
extra payment. The customer can pay through branch cash, debit from an account,
bank transfer, or a digital payment channel. The payment processor calculates
interest, principal, fees, and any late charges. If the payment is partial, the
balance remains open and the schedule is updated. If the payment is full, the
loan is marked as closed and the customer receives a confirmation.

Collections and delinquency management are also part of the lending lifecycle.
If a payment is missed, the system creates a reminder task, sends notifications,
and may apply a policy-driven grace period. Escalation rules describe when the
case moves from reminder to collections, to recovery, and finally to write-off.
Each step must be traceable by the operations team and visible in the audit
trail.

Customer service teams frequently need to open cases about the loan, correct
customer information, freeze a disbursement, reverse a payment, or explain why
a request was rejected. These support flows are not the same as the main
originations flow, but they reuse the same customer, account, loan, payment, and
document entities.

The branch supervisor wants the platform to support a clean "create customer"
flow, a "create loan" flow, a "disburse loan" flow, and a "pay loan" flow.
These flows should be reusable across consumer lending, small business lending,
and refinance products. The same raw notes mention that workflow steps often
invoke document checks, rules validation, and backend services for customer
search, loan calculation, payment posting, and transfer execution.

Operational staff also need a small flow for updating an existing customer:
search the customer, verify identity, confirm the requested change, update the
record, and notify the customer. Another flow handles statement requests and
payment history inquiries. A separate support flow is used when the customer
asks to cancel a pending disbursement before funds are released.

These notes are intentionally unstructured and overlap on purpose, because the
ingestion pipeline should discover the reusable business flows, user tasks, and
tools from the evidence instead of relying on a fixed schema.
